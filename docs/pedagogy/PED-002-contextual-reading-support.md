# PED-002: Contextual help for selected text and vocabulary notes

- Date: 2026-10-11; current state at prompt version `2026-11-26.4`
- Status: implemented; learner usefulness and language quality unvalidated; qualified language review pending
- Scope: reading support in German, Japanese, Russian and Spanish stories
- Evidence basis: product requirements and evidence-informed extrapolation; no direct evaluation of this feature

Interaction details are in [`DESIGN.md`](../../DESIGN.md); pipeline and storage in the [generation pipeline](../architecture/overview.md); Japanese script handling in [japanese.md](../languages/japanese.md).

## Decisions

### Explain selected text

- The learner selects any word, phrase or fragment and asks for a concise English explanation of its contextual meaning and relevant grammar or usage, silently adapted to the learner's level. It covers the exact selection, including fragments within words; surrounding text only clarifies function. Fragments may have no standalone translation.
- Initial help is one short paragraph: no unrelated vocabulary, no teaching of assumed scripts, no profile recap, no assessment.
- The card shows a model-chosen contiguous source excerpt containing the selection, with the exact occurrence highlighted by the app, and an optional short context translation. An invalid excerpt falls back to the containing sentence, dropping any possibly mismatched translation.
- Russian vocabulary notes give stress on the form from the passage as a pronunciation cue; the dictionary form is shown unmarked. They keep the exact inflected form from the passage available for listening and share one lookup identity across stressed and unstressed spellings.

- **More detail** is opt-in (opening the field makes no request) with an optional one-line question; follow-ups stay on the same selection and may build on earlier answers. Plain English paragraphs; light formatting only, model HTML never interpreted. Editing a saved question regenerates its answer from the original explanation and earlier follow-ups. Later answers and examples are removed on save because they may depend on the replaced answer.
- **Generate examples:** three short sentences showing the same meaning or grammatical function in varied contexts, each with a validated highlight and English translation.
- Help stays attached to the exact chapter version and selection for offline reference; saved help is never rewritten.
- **Translate** opens the learner's device translation tool with canonical text (no readings); the external tool chooses languages and may process text online. It does not change the English explanation contract.
- **Listening:** selected text, note headwords and whole chapters can be spoken through Android TTS in the story language, always canonical text without readings, glossary or explanations. Sentence controls are conveniences: Pause stops and Resume repeats the sentence (the host exposes no native pause or word position); sentence boundaries come from `Intl.Segmenter` or punctuation and may differ from a teacher's; one utterance per sentence adds short gaps.

### Vocabulary notes

- Every chapter has a nonempty set of English notes; at least one structurally valid note is required, with no pedagogical quota or cap (the former 100-note cap was removed at the learner's request). Underlines keep help learner-controlled.
- Notes come from a separate request after canonical prose is validated and checkpointed. How many words get a note is a learner setting, **Settings → Vocabulary notes**, which changes only the selection rule of the prompt (the operation saves the choice; the version records it):
  - **Fewer:** only words and expressions likely to be difficult at the selected reading target (less frequent or topic-specific vocabulary, unusual senses, irregular or opaque forms, idioms, set phrases, collocations and constructions not clear from their parts), assuming the learner may have just started that target and skipping only basic words almost certainly met at the level below. This is the 2026-11-26 rule (prompt `2026-11-26.2`).
  - **Standard (default):** near-complete support. With the notes, a learner who has just started the target should understand about 49 of every 50 running words without guessing; the model notes every content word and fixed expression such a learner might not recognize instantly in this sense. At the lowest level (A1, N5) only the skip list counts as known, so Standard equals More there; at higher levels core vocabulary of lower levels in its most common sense is skipped. "49 of 50" is an instruction target taken from lexical-coverage research, not a measurement: the model cannot count known words and the app never claims a coverage figure.
  - **More:** every content word and fixed expression, whatever the level.
  - Standard and More share a **skip list** of closed-class items never noted on their own (articles and determiners, personal and demonstrative pronouns, auxiliary uses of German *sein/haben/werden* and Spanish *haber*, basic negation and yes/no, numerals, common prepositions and conjunctions, Japanese case and topic particles, だ/です and ます endings, routine proper names); their role is explained inside a noted word's note when it matters. Both also name possessive determiners whose reference or form may confuse a beginner (German *ihr/sein*, Spanish *su*, Russian *свой*) and opaque constructions (Russian *У меня есть*, German *es gibt*) as things to note, the construction as one fixed expression.
  - In every setting: duplicate meanings are skipped; each form is one word or a fixed multi-word expression; free combinations of ordinary words (a verb with its regular object or prepositional phrase, such as German *kommt von der Tür*, a noun with its article, a clause) are not noted, only the difficult word inside them. A separable or particle verb split in its sentence (German *Er steht früh auf*) is noted on the attested verb form, with the whole verb explained in the meaning, never with the words between its parts. Which words get notes is the model's estimate from the self-selected level, not a claim that any word is known; any other text stays selectable for Explain and Define.
- Schema: English meaning, headword (`word`, a schema name, not a claim of one orthographic word) and exact `forms`. Forms are resolved tolerantly to attested chapter text; unattested forms are dropped, and a note with none is discarded rather than failing the chapter. Forms only in the title or plan do not count.
- Grouped forms need compatible explanations. The prompt distinguishes referents, person and number, association versus ownership, prefers disambiguating attested phrases, and explains discontinuous constructions without assigning their meaning to an isolated particle.
- Display: each note appears once, at its first eligible occurrence in the displayed version (identity = headword + meaning, NFC, trimmed and collapsed whitespace, case and punctuation preserved; longest overlaps first); later occurrences stay selectable for Explain. Recomputed on render without changing stored data.
- **Define (on-demand notes):** the learner can select any word or expression and tap Define to add a vocabulary note for it. It uses the chapter-note schema and repair, is written for the meaning in this sentence (the dictionary form as headword, a one- or two-sentence English meaning), and must have a form overlapping the selection; a selection inside a word is noted on the whole word, a free combination on its least familiar word. The note is saved in the chapter version's glossary with `addedBy: 'learner'` and displayed exactly like generated notes. Each Define note is anchored to the occurrence it was written for and shown only there, because the same form can mean something else elsewhere in the chapter ("es gibt" versus "sie gibt ihr das Geld"). A selection inside a note already shown on the text opens that note without a request. Define complements Explain: a short reusable gloss rather than an explanation card. Device dictionaries were considered and not used: Android offers no reliable way to find an installed dictionary, and an in-app note keeps the meaning in context and in the learner's lookup history.
- **Notes arrive while reading:** a new chapter opens once its opening paragraph has notes (and, for Japanese, its furigana; later kanji get furigana in smaller blocks alongside the notes), so the first lines read already carry support, and the remaining notes appear as their parts finish, so the learner can start reading before all notes exist. Early on, words may lack underlines that appear moments later; Define works meanwhile and Explain waits until the notes finish. A Define note is kept when later notes arrive and prevails: a generated note whose first occurrence is where the learner defined the word is not shown (it does not move to a later occurrence it was not written for), the same rule as after a notes rebuild. The opening paragraph is its own short notes part (joined with following paragraphs while under half the minimum part size). Assumption: this part is small enough to finish first; limit: the earlier comparison found more errors in ~300-unit parts, so the lead part may be slightly less accurate. Product decision at the learner's request (2026-11-26); not a learning claim.
- **Rebuild vocabulary notes** (reader ⋮ menu): the learner can have the viewed chapter's notes written again with the current Vocabulary notes amount and notes model, for example after changing the amount. Generated notes are replaced on the same version (not a new version, so explanations, readings and reading position stay attached); notes added with Define are kept. The version records the rebuild's prompt version, density and model. Product decision at the learner's request (2026-11-26); not a learning claim.
- Split notes (Settings → Split vocabulary notes, on by default): a long chapter's notes are requested in paragraph parts of similar size, about 1,000 UTF-16 units (400 for denser Japanese; never below 600 or 250), at most 8 parts, of which up to 6 run at once (one at a time with Settings → Allow concurrent requests off). A longer chapter gets more parts rather than larger ones, at the learner's request, because smaller focused parts gave denser coverage than one large request (2026-11-25) and three ~1,200-unit parts worked well for C1 scenes (2026-11-26). Each part receives the whole chapter as context and a `focus` with its paragraphs, and notes only words in its focus. Parts are merged in chapter order; identical notes (same headword and explanation) are joined, different explanations of one headword stay separate. This is a speed decision, not a learning claim. Assumption: whole-chapter context keeps contextual senses as accurate as one request; limit: a word recurring in several parts may get slightly different explanations from different parts, and the display rule below then shows each distinct note once. Revisit if evaluation finds more inconsistent or less accurate notes from split requests.
- Terminology for notes: [lexical-unit review](../research/lexical-units.md).

### Creation and continuation

- Create leads with one combined story idea and topics brief (genre, tone, format, vocabulary domains, situations, grammar concepts, expressions), in English, the target language or both; the app does not promise to correct learner writing. Older stories keep separate topic and practice values.
- Each chapter or rewrite gets its own glossary and carries forward the original topics and learner context. Chapters advance the narrative and leave a natural reason to continue unless closure is requested.
- Blank Continue proceeds naturally; blank Rewrite retells the main events and narrative role. The actual empty prompt is saved; no instructions are invented. Paths: [PED-003](PED-003-story-memory-and-paths.md). Levels and scope: [PED-004](PED-004-level-guided-generation.md).

## Evidence and limits

- **Self-report** informs adaptation but does not establish proficiency (`liZhang2021SelfAssessment`, `tigchelaarEtAl2017CanDoValidity`; [self-assessment review](../research/self-assessment.md)).
- **Glossed reading** has support with heterogeneous, partial outcomes ([reading and glossing review](../research/reading-and-glossing.md)). `yanagisawaWebbUchihara2020Glossing` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263119000688)) supports offering contextual help as an option; it does not test first-occurrence display, these languages or AI explanations, and does not fix an optimal frequency.
- **Lexical coverage** motivates the Standard target: comprehension rises roughly linearly with the share of known words, with about 95% often cited as a minimum and 98% as adequate (`lauferRavenhorstKalovski2010LexicalThreshold`, `schmittJiangGrabe2011PercentageKnown`); a partial replication found only 100% significantly better than 90–95% (`webbEtAl2025CoverageRevisited`), and for beginning Spanish learners 98% predicted recall of most main events (`hermanLeeser2022LexicalCoverage`). These studies manipulate or measure known words; they do not show that notes substitute for known words, and the app does not measure coverage. See the [reading and glossing review](../research/reading-and-glossing.md#e5--lexical-coverage-and-gloss-density).
- **Gloss density** has no established optimum: a meta-analysis found the largest comprehension effects with heavy computer-based glossing but the second largest with 5% or less (`taylor2014GlossingFrequency`), and dense glossing drew attention from unglossed words without a comprehension trade-off (`majuddin2014GlossDensity`). Tap-to-open notes may limit overload; that is a product assumption. The setting exists because the right density varies by learner.
- **Reading-first practice** has small-to-medium, possibly overestimated support, mostly for sustained classroom reading, not one-off AI chapters: `sangersEtAl2025ExtensiveReading` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-025-10068-6)); `hamada2020ExtensiveReadingBias` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00617)) lowered one estimate after correcting selection bias.
- **Repeated encounters** correlate with incidental learning without a required count (`uchiharaEtAl2019Repetition`, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12343)), so natural reuse remains a product assumption.
- **Product assumptions** (no direct evidence): focused and silently adapted help, three examples, first-occurrence display, the three note densities, the skip list and the "49 of 50" wording, no density cap, Listen and Translate, sentence playback controls, story-led creation, multilingual input, open-ended endings, and all presentation choices.
- Structural validation cannot establish semantic accuracy, useful coverage, level suitability or pedagogical value; language review is separate. The cause of reported Android failures with prose-prefixed detail responses was never confirmed from a captured response.
- Retrieval, review and instruction are not implemented; they are research candidates in the [future directions](../product/future-directions.md).

## Implementation

- Code: `js/app.js` (reader, help requests), `js/reader-text.js` (canonical text, glossary display), `js/learning-validation.js` (validation), `js/prompts.js` (notes prompt `NOTES_INSTRUCTIONS`, help prompts `helpInstructions`).
- Provenance: saved help records passage offsets, source version, profile snapshot, model and prompt version; follow-ups add question, response and their own provenance; legacy JSON detail objects remain readable. Prompt or learning-behavior changes increment `PROMPT_VERSION`; copy and presentation changes do not; saved records never relabel.
- Lookups of notes, explanations, follow-ups, examples and translations are recorded under [PED-005](PED-005-consultation-signals.md); recording changes no help content or prompt.

## Open evaluation

A controlled contextual-accuracy comparison of notes prompts is designed but not run: 24 frozen revised-pilot chapters plus seven focused cases. Promotion requires qualified adjudication, fewer material errors overall, no increase per language or in missing useful support, correct focused outputs, and a new `PROMPT_VERSION`; AI preference alone is insufficient. See the [comparison design](../evaluation/generation-tests/notes-prompt-experiment/README.md) and [generation evaluation](../evaluation/generation-tests/method.md).

## Revisit when

- Qualified language review or learner evaluation finds notes or explanations confuse learners or do not support comprehension.
- Android testing shows visible furigana blocks reliable selection of base text (disabling furigana is not an acceptable workaround).

- 2026-11-26: a new chapter opens only once its opening paragraph has notes; up to six parts run at once unless Allow concurrent requests is off.
- 2026-11-26: at the learner's request, the reader menu gained Rebuild vocabulary notes for the viewed chapter (generated notes replaced in place, Define notes kept), and Settings gained a choice of which tasks use the light model (explanations and examples now may). Settings were then reorganized: AI models (story model with its effort, and a collapsed Light model row), Vocabulary notes, While you read, and Advanced last.
