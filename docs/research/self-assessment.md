# Reading self-assessment

- **Question:** Can learners describe their current German, Japanese, Russian or Spanish reading ability well enough to guide generated stories?
- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-10 (follow-up the same day). Scoped review via Consensus.
- **Access:** Consensus records and abstracts; no independent full-text review. Bibliographic metadata for Brown et al., Tigchelaar et al. (Wiley pages) and Luchkina et al. ([publisher PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/languages/languages-06-00083/article_deploy/languages-06-00083.pdf)) was checked; that is not a methodological review.
- **Informs:** [PED-001](../pedagogy/PED-001-learner-self-report.md) (superseded), [PED-004](../pedagogy/PED-004-level-guided-generation.md).

## Conclusion

Short, concrete reading can-do descriptions are a reasonable revisable starting point for low-stakes personalization. Self-report correlates moderately with performance, varies with experience and item wording, and differs by skill. It must not be scored, mapped to CEFR/JLPT/TORFL, or presented as overall proficiency; the app asks only about reading because it offers reading. Free text alone is not an established placement instrument. Script literacy (kana, Cyrillic) is a product entry assumption, not a finding. No direct Spanish self-assessment evidence was found; German appears only in Ashton (2014).

## Sources

1. **Li & Zhang (2021; online 2020)**, "A meta-analysis of self-assessment and language performance in language testing and assessment", *Language Testing* 38, 189–218. `liZhang2021SelfAssessment`, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265532220932481). 67 studies, 97 independent samples, >68,500 participants; overall self-assessment/performance r = .466, moderated by criteria, instruments and training. Supports useful but imperfect self-report; does not validate app labels or free text.
2. **Ashton (2014)**, "Using self-assessment to compare learners' reading proficiency in a multilingual assessment framework", *System* 42, 105–119. `ashton2014ReadingSelfAssessment`, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.system.2013.11.006). Secondary-school reading self-assessment in German, Japanese and Urdu (England): similar factors across languages, differences in how statements represented progression. Supports language-specific reading descriptors; not adults, kanji thresholds or generated stories.
3. **Brown, Dewey & Cox (2014)**, "Assessing the validity of can-do statements in retrospective (then-now) self-assessment", *Foreign Language Annals* 47, 261–285. `brownDeweyCox2014CanDoValidity`, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/flan.12082). Internship abroad in Russia: reliable self-assessment, only moderate correlation with oral proficiency interviews; ACTFL-level item-group means not significantly different. Retrospective and speaking, not current reading.
4. **Luchkina, Ionin, Lysenko, Stoops & Suvorkina (2021)**, "Evaluating the Russian language proficiency of bilingual and second language learners of Russian", *Languages* 6, 83. `luchkinaEtAl2021RussianProficiency`, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/languages6020083). Self-report, background questionnaire and a validated cloze test; 52 baseline and 503 bilingual/multilingual speakers. Validity of that cloze does not transfer to an improvised quiz or our bands.
5. **Suzuki (2015)**, "Self-assessment of Japanese as a second language: The role of experiences in the naturalistic acquisition", *Language Testing* 32, 63–81. `suzuki2015JapaneseSelfAssessment`, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265532214541885). Self-assessment error larger for tasks with little experience. Motivates asking about things actually understood.
6. **Tigchelaar, Bowles, Winke & Gass (2017)**, "Assessing the validity of ACTFL can-do statements for spoken proficiency: A Rasch analysis", *Foreign Language Annals* 50, 584–600. `tigchelaarEtAl2017CanDoValidity`, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/flan.12286). 382 undergraduate Spanish learners; 15 of 50 speaking items misfit (suspected vagueness, unfamiliar experiences, multiple skills per item). Informs concrete single-task wording.

## Applied design (2026-10-10, now superseded)

Three bands per language with language-specific descriptions (Japanese: short sentences with kanji readings vs connected or nuanced text; Russian: short everyday sentences vs connected stories), plus optional English first-person examples of familiar reading tasks and support needs, with no grammar lists or band restatements. Each request carried the band, self-assessment and expectations, saved with prompt version `2026-10-10.7`. PED-004 replaced the bands with standardized self-selected targets on 2026-10-13; the evidence limits above still apply.

## Open validation

Qualified language review of descriptors and generated stories in all four languages. If accurate placement becomes necessary, evaluate a validated reading measure for the intended population rather than an invented questionnaire.
