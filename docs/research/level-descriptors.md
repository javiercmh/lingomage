# Language level descriptions for story generation

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-10
**Question:** What evidence supports short Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced descriptions for German, Japanese, Russian, and Spanish, and how strongly may they be presented?

## Evidence reviewed

- Malin Ågren, Camilla Bardel, and Susan Sayehli (2025), [“Developing measures of beginner language proficiency in French, German, and Spanish in the Swedish school context”](https://consensus.app/papers/developing-measures-of-beginner-language-proficiency-in-ågren-bardel/83e87b33e1105c5195c439cf682c293f/?utm_source=chatgpt), *The Language Learning Journal* (1 citation in Consensus). DOI: [10.1080/09571736.2025.2564704](https://doi.org/10.1080/09571736.2025.2564704). Consensus record/abstract reviewed. A written C-test was developed for 144 Swedish 15-year-olds learning French, German, or Spanish. It was sensitive to variation among beginner learners; interpretation across language groups changed with scoring method. The sample and test concern school learners and written German/Spanish/French, not story generation, Chilean Spanish, Japanese, or Russian.
- J. Hulstijn (2014), [“The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: A challenge for applied linguistics”](https://consensus.app/papers/the-common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-a-hulstijn/fcfc42a77563585bb9c5de62648ebb4d/?utm_source=chatgpt), *ITL – International Journal of Applied Linguistics*, 165, 3–18 (65 citations in Consensus). DOI: [10.1075/itl.165.1.01hul](https://doi.org/10.1075/itl.165.1.01hul). Consensus record/abstract reviewed. This critical analysis argues that CEFR levels lack empirical support from language-use data and identifies possible mismatches between proficiency levels, development, intellectual ability, and communicative versus linguistic competence. It cautions against treating broad level labels as validated measurements.
- W. Tominaga (2014), [“Validating the scoring inference of the Japanese OPI ratings: The use of extended turns, connective expressions, and discourse organization”](https://consensus.app/papers/validating-the-scoring-inference-of-the-japanese-opi-tominaga/f1d5fb9897375af09c94390318fc436c/?utm_source=chatgpt) (1 citation in Consensus). Consensus record/abstract reviewed. Conversation analysis of 15 Japanese Oral Proficiency Interviews found that higher-level candidates tended to use connective expressions more effectively, while text-type descriptions did not always match candidate performance, especially at lower intermediate levels. The sample is small and concerns oral interviews, not reading stories.
- Sanae Eda, Masayuki Itomitsu, and Mari Noda (2008), [“The Japanese Skills Test as an On-Demand Placement Test: Validity Comparisons and Reliability”](https://consensus.app/papers/the-japanese-skills-test-as-an-on‐demand-placement-test-eda-itomitsu/618cf863c8115849a3e0cf10467718ea/?utm_source=chatgpt), *Foreign Language Annals*, 41, 218–236 (9 citations in Consensus). DOI: [10.1111/j.1944-9720.2008.tb03290.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1944-9720.2008.tb03290.x). Consensus record/abstract reviewed. The study reports validity and reliability evidence for a Japanese placement test in a nine-week intensive higher-education program, particularly for distinguishing first- and second-year ability. It evaluates a specific test and context, not three general story-writing bands.

## Decision

Settings offer three practical controls named Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced. Each language has its own short English description that asks the generator for observable text features (familiarity and concreteness, sentence connection and common forms, or broader structures and more precise vocabulary). Japanese descriptions explicitly account for kana/kanji and readings; Russian preserves Cyrillic; Spanish uses broadly understandable usage, with regional preference left to the learner.

The descriptors are informed by the general idea of describing observable language tasks, with a language-specific design assumption. Evidence reviewed here does **not** establish that these three bands correspond to validated proficiency levels, predict learner ability, or cause learning gains. Direct evidence is especially limited for Japanese and absent here for Russian and Spanish. Do not call the labels CEFR-aligned or infer a learner's level from a selected setting. A generation-level override is a writing preference, not an assessment.

## Product implications and open questions

- Keep the three level descriptions editable as product copy in `js/app.js`; review with qualified speakers and target learners before claiming quality or effectiveness.
- Preserve selected language and difficulty in each saved story's provenance. Existing stories remain unchanged.
- Future validation should assess generated language separately by language and task, with human review and explicit criteria. A proficiency test or learning-effectiveness claim needs its own suitable study.
- Consensus abstracts and metadata were reviewed; full text was not independently inspected for these records.
