# Level descriptions for story generation

- **Question:** What evidence supports short level descriptions for German, Japanese, Russian and Spanish, and how strongly may they be presented?
- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-10. Scoped review via Consensus.
- **Access:** Consensus records and abstracts; full text not inspected.
- **Informs:** [PED-001](../pedagogy/PED-001-learner-self-report.md), [PED-004](../pedagogy/PED-004-level-guided-generation.md).

## Conclusion

Describing observable text features per level (familiarity and concreteness; sentence connection and common forms; broader structures and precise vocabulary) is a reasonable design assumption. The evidence does not show that any set of app bands corresponds to validated proficiency levels, predicts ability or causes learning; CEFR levels themselves have contested empirical grounding. Direct evidence is limited for Japanese and absent here for Russian and Spanish. Never call app labels certified-aligned or infer a learner's level from a selection. Generated language must be evaluated per language and task with human review.

## Sources

1. **Ågren, Bardel & Sayehli (2025)**, "Developing measures of beginner language proficiency in French, German, and Spanish in the Swedish school context", *The Language Learning Journal*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/09571736.2025.2564704). Written C-test for 144 Swedish 15-year-olds; sensitive to variation among beginners; cross-language interpretation changed with scoring method. School learners and written tests, not generation, Japanese or Russian.
2. **Hulstijn (2014)**, "The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: A challenge for applied linguistics", *ITL – International Journal of Applied Linguistics* 165, 3–18. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1075/itl.165.1.01hul). Argues CEFR levels lack empirical support from language-use data and conflate proficiency, development, intellectual ability and communicative vs linguistic competence. Caution against treating labels as measurements.
3. **Tominaga (2014)**, "Validating the scoring inference of the Japanese OPI ratings: The use of extended turns, connective expressions, and discourse organization" ([record](https://consensus.app/papers/validating-the-scoring-inference-of-the-japanese-opi-tominaga/f1d5fb9897375af09c94390318fc436c/)). Conversation analysis of 15 Japanese OPIs: higher levels used connectives more effectively; text-type descriptions did not always match performance, especially lower intermediate. Small oral sample.
4. **Eda, Itomitsu & Noda (2008)**, "The Japanese Skills Test as an on-demand placement test: Validity comparisons and reliability", *Foreign Language Annals* 41, 218–236. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1944-9720.2008.tb03290.x). Validity and reliability for one placement test in a nine-week intensive program (first vs second year). A specific test and context.
