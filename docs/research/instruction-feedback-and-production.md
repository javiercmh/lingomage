# Explicit instruction, corrective feedback, listening and production tasks

Review date: 2026-10-19. Scoped, not systematic. Discovery used Consensus search; metadata was checked in Crossref; original text was read only where noted. Access levels are defined in [retrieval and spacing](retrieval-and-spacing.md#access-levels-used) (full text summarized, abstract + metadata, provisional).

**Questions.** What does published evidence say about explicit instruction, corrective feedback, story-linked listening and learner production (speaking, writing, dialogue with an AI)? What would transfer to a reading-first app whose AI output is unvalidated?

**Status.** Contextual explanations exist ([PED-002](../pedagogy/PED-002-contextual-reading-support.md)); chapter listening uses native speech. There is no instruction sequence, learner production task or feedback feature. These are [Horizon 3](../product/evidence-informed-strategy.md#horizon-3-explore-story-linked-listening-writing-and-feedback) candidates.

## Explicit instruction

`liSun2024ExplicitInstruction` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/flan.12726); Foreign Language Annals 57(1); online 2023): abstract + metadata. Meta-analysis of 28 reports (67 samples, N = 3,754). Reported moderate-to-large effects (d = 1.07 within-group, 0.81 between-group), with instruction type, practice type, delivery mode, treatment length, outcome-measure type and reporting of measure validity moderating results.

Limits: outcome-measure type moderated the effects, and the abstract does not say how much of the benefit appears on delayed or spontaneous-use measures (a general caution, not a finding of this study). The abstract does not say explanations should replace language use, and it does not evaluate AI-written explanations or reading-embedded notes. Current on-demand explanations are help, not an instructional program.

## Corrective feedback

- `li2010CorrectiveFeedback` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x); Language Learning 60(2)): abstract + metadata. 33 studies. Medium overall effect, maintained over time; implicit feedback better maintained than explicit. Lab studies, shorter treatments and foreign-language settings produced larger effects than classroom studies, longer treatments and second-language settings. The abstract reports these as separate moderator contrasts; whether they are confounded with one another was not examined, so the "implicit is better maintained" result is not generalized here.
- `lysterSaito2010OralFeedback` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263109990520); SSLA 32(2)): abstract + metadata. 15 classroom studies, N = 827. Significant, durable effects; prompts outperformed recasts, especially on free-response measures; younger learners benefited more.

These syntheses concern human-delivered feedback; Li includes laboratory and classroom settings, while Lyster and Saito focus on classroom oral feedback. Neither establishes the effectiveness of automated written feedback on learner writing. Their feedback categories and settings differ, so the results do not establish a universal preferred feedback form.

## Listening and reading-while-listening

`huiGodfroid2026ReadingWhileListening` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12721); Language Learning 76(1); registered report): abstract + metadata. 86 intermediate-to-advanced Chinese learners of English. Contrary to preregistration, comprehension was lower when reading while listening than when reading silently; both reading conditions beat listening alone. `webbEtAl2023IncidentalMetaAnalysis` found similar partial incidental vocabulary gains for reading, listening and reading-while-listening, with high heterogeneity. Consensus also returned other single studies favoring reading-while-listening for vocabulary; those abstracts were not opened and are not relied on.

Applicability: advanced Chinese learners of English and one text type. Findings do not cover beginners, Japanese or Russian script, or synthesized speech. Whether the device's native speech output helps comprehension in this app is untested. Treat story-linked listening as exploratory.

## Task-based and dialogue-based production

- `bryfonskiMcKay2019TBLT` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1362168817744389); LTR 23(5)): abstract + metadata. 52 studies of long-term TBLT *program* implementation; reported d = 0.93. This is program-level evaluation, not a measure of any single conversation or writing task.
- `xuanEtAl2025TBLTComment` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688221131127)): abstract + metadata. Reanalysis with stricter criteria reported a smaller effect (g = 0.61) and fewer acceptable studies, urging caution.
- `boersFaez2026TBLTMetaAnalysis` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688231167573)): abstract + metadata. Concludes the field is not ready for a meaningful meta-analysis of the *relative* effectiveness of task-based or task-supported programs, citing unclear definitions of "task".
- `houMin2026DialogueCALL` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344025100268); ReCALL 38(1)): open-access page, summarized full text. 16 studies, 89 effect sizes, g = .61 for speaking. Significant moderators: system type, meaning constraint, modality. Most systems were not generative AI (stated in the article), and no comparison with human tutors is reported. Corrective feedback and proficiency were not significant moderators.
- `bibauwEtAl2022DialogueSystems` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.64152/10125/73488); LLT 26(1)): abstract + metadata. 17 studies, d = 0.58, with larger effects for form-focused, goal-oriented, system-guided designs and for lower-proficiency learners.

Net: dialogue-based practice has a reasonable body of controlled studies, but mostly pre-LLM, English-target systems. The TBLT figure is disputed. None tests story-linked tasks, AI scoring validity or retention in the four target languages.

Outcome and transfer evidence for repeated-task speaking and its limits, and the d/g comparison caution for the TBLT figures, are in [outcomes and measurement](outcomes-and-measurement.md).

## What this does not support

No source compares a combined approach with single methods across skills. A single small vocabulary study found bundled retrieval with spacing beat restudy (`karatasEtAl2025MultidomainVocabulary`), which is not evidence that mixing input, instruction, production and feedback is superior overall. LLM-generated feedback or scoring reliability was not reviewed here; the shared conversation did not cite any, and AI scoring would still not demonstrate learning.
