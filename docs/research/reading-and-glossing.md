# Reading, glossary support, and repeated encounters

Review date: 2026-10-09. Discovery: Consensus; publication metadata and available
original text checked separately. This is a scoped initial review, not a systematic review.

## E1 — Meaning-focused vocabulary learning

Webb, S., Uchihara, T., & Yanagisawa, A. (2023). *How effective is second language
incidental vocabulary learning? A meta-analysis*. Language Teaching, 56, 161–180.
[DOI: 10.1017/S0261444822000507](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444822000507).

Access: original full-text methods and results inspected. Synthesis of 24 studies,
29 effect sizes, and 2,771 participants, covering reading, listening, combined
input, and viewing. Learners and proficiency levels varied.

Meaning-focused input supported vocabulary gains, but learning rates were partial
and heterogeneous. Reading yielded mean target-word learning rates of 17% on
immediate and 15% on delayed tests. Different assessments measure different
knowledge; those percentages are not predictions for an individual learner.

Decision: offer short stories as reading practice, not as guaranteed mastery.
Learner-oriented text and simple language are reasonable starting constraints.
AI generation, four specific target languages, and the selected story length were
not evaluated as LingoMage's intervention; transfer remains an assumption.

## E2 — Glossing

Yanagisawa, A., Webb, S., & Uchihara, T. (2020). *How do different forms of glossing
contribute to L2 vocabulary learning from reading?* Studies in Second Language
Acquisition, 42, 411–438.
[DOI: 10.1017/S0272263119000688](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263119000688).

Access: original publisher abstract inspected; full methods not reviewed.
Meta-analysis of 42 studies, 359 effect sizes, and 3,802 participants comparing
gloss formats and glossed versus nonglossed reading.

Glossed reading supported greater learning; L1 glosses outperformed L2 glosses in
the synthesis. However, glossaries were among the weaker formats, and learning
varied with assessment, comprehension, and proficiency.

Decision: retain the requested English contextual glossary for English-speaking
learners. Do not call it the most effective format. Glossary placement and future
interactive formats remain design questions. These studies do not validate AI
translations or guarantee that English is every user's first language.

## E3 — Repeated encounters

Uchihara, T., Webb, S., & Yanagisawa, A. (2019). *The effects of repetition on
incidental vocabulary learning: A meta-analysis of correlational studies*.
Language Learning, 69, 559–599.
[DOI: 10.1111/lang.12343](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12343).

Access: original publisher abstract inspected. Synthesis of 26 studies,
45 effect sizes, and 1,918 participants examining encounters and learning.
The reported association was moderate (r = .34), with learner, treatment, and
measurement moderators.

Decision: request natural vocabulary reuse and allow another story from the same
words. This correlational synthesis does not establish a required repetition
count or a causal effect of LingoMage's regeneration feature. Avoid forcing a
fixed encounter count that makes stories unnatural.

## Product assumptions and remaining review

Five to ten words, story lengths, A1-oriented AI suggestions, glossary placement,
and local rereading are product choices. No learning trial has been run.
Language-specific grammar progression and beginner readability still need deeper
review; content checks must not be reported as retention evidence.
