# Retention, transfer, spontaneous production and measurement

Review date: 2026-10-19. Scoped, not systematic. Discovery and contrary-evidence searches used Consensus; bibliographic metadata and abstract summaries were checked in Crossref, OpenAlex or the publisher page; Consensus is discovery assistance, not evidence. Access levels follow [retrieval and spacing](retrieval-and-spacing.md#access-levels-used); "Provisional" means the detail was seen only in a secondary record.

**Questions.** (1) What do published studies show about retention, transfer to untrained material and spontaneous production, and how far do their measures support those labels? (2) What do measurement studies imply for any future in-app evaluation? Intended outcomes are defined in [learning outcomes and evaluation](../product/learning-outcomes-and-evaluation.md); this note holds only the evidence.

**Status.** The proposed assessment capability is not implemented. No source below evaluates LingoMage or establishes effectiveness across its four target languages; some individual studies involve one of those languages.

## Retention and delayed testing

`webbEtAl2020IntentionalActivities` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12671); abstract-level, see its bibliography note): pooled average gains from word-focused activities were about 60% on immediate recall and lower on delayed recall, with a larger drop for form recall than meaning recall. These are study-level means across different designs, so they are not within-learner retention rates. `webbEtAl2023IncidentalMetaAnalysis`: incidental reading gains of 17% immediate and 15% delayed (proportions of target words learned, pooled), partial and heterogeneous. Neither shows spontaneous use.

## Transfer to unseen material

- `barenbergEtAl2021TestingTransfer` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.3796); abstract summary): 182 eighth-grade students, English vocabulary, quasi-experimental; testing beat restudy overall and in three of four final-test formats; effects were smaller when the final test differed from the initial test. A young-learner school population, not adults reading stories.
- `vandenBroekEtAl2018ContextRetrieval` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12285); abstract summary): three experiments on novel words; informative context helped comprehension during practice, but retrieval practice (with or without feedback) gave better later recall and recognition, including recognition in a new context. Comprehension during practice and retention differ, which is why they must be measured separately.
- Both are word-level. Recognition in a new sentence context is a narrow form of transfer; it does not establish whole-passage comprehension or spontaneous use in unfamiliar communicative tasks.

## Spontaneous production

- `lambertKormosMinn2017TaskRepetition` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263116000085); Cambridge abstract page): 32 Japanese learners of English at three proficiency levels repeated three oral monologue tasks (instruction, narration, opinion) six times. Immediate same-task repetition was related to gains in speech rate and in specific pause and repair measures, with the largest speech-rate gains over the first three performances. The abstract concerns repeated performances of the same task; it does not report a delayed or novel-task test. Rehearsed repetition is not evidence of spontaneous use.
- `kimTracyVentura2013TaskRepetition` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.system.2013.08.005)): Crossref metadata confirmed. **Provisional design details** (secondary records): 36 Korean junior high students; same-task-and-content repetition versus same-procedure-with-new-content over about four weeks with a pretest and two posttests; procedural repetition promoted syntactic complexity; neither group had a significant speech-rate gain. A young, non-adult, one-language-pair sample; do not generalize to adults.
- `suzuki2021BlockedInterleaved` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12433)): a posttest with new cartoon narratives favored blocked over interleaved repetition for articulation rate and mid-clause pauses, while training-session gains did not translate into meaningful pre-post change on some measures. `suzukiHanzawa2022MassedRepetition` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263121000358)): massed repetition reduced pausing on an immediate new-cartoon posttest but slowed articulation and increased verbatim repetition; schedule differences mostly disappeared on a one-week new-cartoon posttest. Both use picture narratives, a narrow task type, and fluency indices, not communicative success.
- `li2010CorrectiveFeedback`: see [instruction, feedback and production](instruction-feedback-and-production.md#corrective-feedback).

Summary: the strongest evidence here supports gains on repeated or closely related tasks; evidence for durable gains on unrelated spontaneous communication is thin and mixed. This is the core generalization gap in the supplied excerpt, and the reviewed abstracts do not close it.

## Measurement evidence

- **Test-taking can affect learning.** `puimegeEtAl2025Pretesting` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263125100910); abstract summary): 143 adults; pretests (meaning recall, meaning recognition, form recognition) had a small, non-significant effect on posttest scores of incidental vocabulary from reading. So a baseline probe may have little influence on reading-based gains in that design, but the result is for one task, not a guarantee. Same-item retests and testing effects (retrieval is itself learning, see [retrieval and spacing](retrieval-and-spacing.md)) must still be treated as possible contamination, and a general pretesting literature exists that was not reviewed here.
- **AI scoring is conditional.** `alghamdiAlghizzi2026LLMAssessment` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/frma.2026.1908592); open-access full text, summarized): 52 studies (2022–2025). Human-LLM score correspondence was moderate (r = 0.66, 95% CI 0.53 to 0.75) from only three comparable studies, with substantial heterogeneity; formative feedback gave a small-to-moderate effect on revision quality and short-term outcomes (g = 0.42, 14 studies). The authors conclude evidence supports bounded, human-supervised uses, not autonomous high-stakes scoring. Prompt text was missing in 60% of studies and model versions in 27%, so prompt and model provenance must be recorded. Evidence is mostly university English writing, not Japanese or Russian.
- **Engagement is not learning.** `hiverDao2025TaskEngagement` ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.3410); abstract summary): 106 Vietnamese learners of English; self-reported task engagement followed task motivation, discourse-based engagement indicators did not, and discourse measures predicted later learning better than self-reports. Self-reported enjoyment or time spent cannot stand in for learning.
- **Fluency indices are not communicative success.** The production studies above use speed, pause and complexity indices; none of the reviewed abstracts reports communicative success or comprehensibility as an outcome.

## Applicability and gaps

Direct evidence for LingoMage: none. Extrapolation: reading-based retention and recognition tests (word-level) are plausible first targets; novel-reading comprehension is a reading-first outcome that none of the reviewed studies measures after AI-generated stories. Gaps: delays of months, typed production tasks for German, Japanese and Russian, beginner floor effects, AI scoring validated per language, and reliability of self-administered probes. Unresolved: whether optional in-app probes would change reading behavior.
